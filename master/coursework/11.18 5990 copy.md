Waterfall 采用线性、顺序式流程，需求一旦确定后难以更改，灵活性低。

Waterfall 在前期需求阶段之后，客户参与有限；

Agile 在每个迭代都有持续反馈、评审会议和产品演示，利益相关者参与度高。

Flexibility:

Waterfall follows a linear, sequential model where changes are difficult once requirements are set.

Agile uses short iterations that allow continuous adjustment, making it highly flexible.

Stakeholder involvement:

In Waterfall, stakeholders participate mainly at the beginning and end.

In Agile, stakeholders are engaged throughout each sprint through reviews and feedback sessions.

History of ITSM

1960s: IT was primarily hardware-centric, support was ad hoc and there was no formal process. 1980s: Organizations realize that IT needs to be reliable and standardized, and the UK government creates ITIL v1. 1990s: ITIL v2 becomes the global standard for ITSM best practices. 2000s: ITIL v3 introduces a service lifecycle model, and ITSM goes beyond IT support to become business-aligned IT delivery. 2010s: ITIL 4 integrates agile principles to make ITSM more flexible. Today: ITSM now focuses on cloud services, automation and AI, user experience

ITIL v3 process

1.Service strategy: Define IT as a strategic service provider that delivers value.2.Service design: designing IT services and processes to meet business needs.3.Service Transformation: Securely build, test, and deploy IT services.4.Service Operations: Efficiently operate and support IT services.5.Continuous Service Improvement: Continuously measure and improve services based on feedback and performance metrics

Precautions: Encrypt data at rest and in transit; Implement strong access controls (least privilege, RBAC); regular safety awareness training; patch and vulnerability management; Continuous monitoring and auditing systems; Back up critical data and test recovery plans

Cybercrime #6 - Phishing and Social Engineering Social engineering: manipulating or deceiving people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security; Phishing: A type of social engineering that uses emails, messages, or websites to trick users into providing sensitive data; Key idea: attackers exploit human psychology, not just technical vulnerabilities; Preventive Measures: User Awareness and Training: Teach employees to recognize suspicious emails, links, and messages; Multi-factor authentication (MFA): Prevents stolen credentials from being sufficient to access accounts; Email and web filters: Block malicious content before it reaches users

Cybercrime #7 - DDoS Attacks: Definition: Distributed denial-of-service attacks aim to overwhelm a target (server, service, network) with traffic or requests from many distributed sources, making them inaccessible to legitimate users; The goal is destruction rather than data theft (although DDoS is sometimes used as a smokescreen for other attacks). Preventive measures: Traffic scrubbing/scrubbing center (third-party DDoS mitigation service); rate limiting and upstream filtering in collaboration with ISPs; autoscale with discreet limits; load balancers and redundancy across regions; Centralized logging, SIEM, and real-time traffic monitoring

Cybercrime #8 - Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs)/Advanced Persistent Threats: Definition: Long-term and targeted cyberattacks in which an intruder gains unauthorized access to a network and remains undetected for an extended period of time; APTs often target high-value assets such as intellectual property, financial data, or government secrets. Preventive measures: Continuous network monitoring, anomaly detection, endpoint detection; Close known security vulnerabilities that APTs may exploit; detailed plans for detecting, containment, and remediation of intrusions; Deploy least privilege, network segmentation, and multi-factor authentication

1. Data Lifecycle生命周期:Introduction: The data lifecycle is a fundamental concept in information security and data governance. It shows how data is created, used, stored, and ultimately disposed of; Understanding this is crucial for IT professionals as security controls must be applied at every stage; Data is not static - it goes through stages. Common models have 6-7 stages (some frameworks are slightly different).

a) Explain how BI architecture components (data sources, ETL, warehouse, OLAP) work together.（3 marks）
